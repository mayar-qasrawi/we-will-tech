## 2. Main content — `main` — “The Triad Quality Framework”

- **TEXT** `div.blog-card-meta` — Apr 3, 2024
- **H1** — The Triad Quality Framework
- **P** — A useful quality strategy balances three dimensions together: user value, system integrity, and business alignment. Neglecting one dimension weakens the whole release.
- **IMG** — alt: "Abstract purple WE WILL blog cover" — `/uploads/f069d4b8556d006dd972fadc.png`
- **P** — The Triad Quality Framework describes quality as a balance across three dimensions. A product is not truly ready just because it works in isolation. It must create user value, preserve system integrity, and support the business outcome behind the release.
- **H2** — User value
- **P** — This dimension asks whether the product solves the right problem in the right way for the user. A technically correct feature can still fail if it creates confusion, friction, or weak outcomes in real usage.
- **H2** — System integrity
- **P** — This dimension focuses on stability, resilience, compatibility, and the reliability of core behavior under pressure. It is where defects, risky edge cases, and integration failures usually surface first.
- **H2** — Business alignment
- **P** — This dimension checks whether the release supports timing, risk appetite, and business priorities. Quality work needs to clarify tradeoffs so stakeholders can decide with evidence instead of guesswork.
- **H2** — Use the framework to shape testing
- **P** — The framework is most useful when it drives scenario selection and release conversations. Instead of testing randomly, teams can build focused coverage around what matters to the user, the system, and the business.
