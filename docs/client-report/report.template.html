A quick word before you dive in

This report is a guided tour of your website's new structure. We'll walk you through why each page is there, what people see when they land on it, and how we've set it up so the right buyers can find you on Google. Whenever a technical term pops up, we'll tell you what it actually means for you.

In short: your website now tells one clear story. WE WILL Technology is an Agentic Software Quality company that helps product teams know what is safe to ship, at the speed they build. Vibe Test is the lead product, and it now lives on wewill.tech itself. Business Care Quality (BCQ) is the methodology behind it, and BCQ Expert Services add human judgment when a release carries real risk. Every page, from the main menu to the last button, leads to one next step: Run a Guided Vibe Test.

Section 3 explained the thinking behind each page. This section is your handy reference: the actual titles, descriptions and settings on every page, taken from the finished build on 30 September 2026.

A few words to help you read the tables

The meta title is the clickable blue headline people see in Google, and the meta description is the short summary underneath. Google usually shows around 60 characters of a title and around 155 of a description. The main heading (H1) is the page's biggest headline, and every page should have exactly one. Links pointing here counts how many links across your 11 pages lead to each page, which is a good sign of how central it is.

One note about keywords: Google stopped using the "keywords" tag to rank pages a long time ago, so we didn't add one. Your buyers' vocabulary does its work where Google actually reads it: the description, the headings and the words on the page.

Section 7

Arabic and English

Every page, including the new Vibe Test page, switches between English and Arabic with the EN | AR button in the header, and the whole layout flips to read right to left. The new copy has Arabic throughout, and we ran a terminology review so technical words are used consistently. For example, a release (a version, الإصدار) is kept distinct from a launch (going live, الإطلاق); QA is ضمان الجودة; BCQ is جودة رعاية الأعمال; and GO / NO-GO is spelled one way everywhere. We'd still ask a native-speaking member of your team to give the Arabic a final read before launch.

One important SEO point for Saudi Arabia. Today Arabic is switched inside the browser on the same web address, so Google only ever sees, and ranks, the English version. Since Saudi Arabia is your priority market, we recommend giving Arabic its own addresses (for example wewill.tech/ar/solutions/), linked to their English twins with "hreflang" tags. That tells Google which language version to show to which searcher, so Arabic searches can find you too. It's a platform decision for your team.
